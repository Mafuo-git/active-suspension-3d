Try this

  • Stiffen the rear anti-roll bar: the balance moves to oversteer and the rear tyres turn red first.
  • Set the front camber to 0° in the lab: the loaded front tyre leans out and the car understeers.
  • Move the aero balance rearwards: stable in fast corners, lazy in slow ones.
  • Turn off ABS and brake hard while turning: locked fronts, no steering.
  • Raise the front roll centre (lab, Points tab): less body roll, more front load transfer.
  • Turn off the steering assist and steer hard: the front slip angle goes past its peak (≈7.5°) and the car pushes wide. Less steering is often faster.
  • Put two wheels on the grass: about 40 % of the grip and a lot of drag, then dirty tyres for the next few hundred metres.

Track test

Drive the car with the setup from the suspension lab and watch how it behaves. The car is simulated at full size (lab model ×4). The suspension kinematics run live: body roll and pitch change each wheel's camber and steer angle, which changes its grip.

Reverse: once the car is stopped, release the brake and press it again. The brake then drives backwards and the throttle stops the car; press the throttle at a standstill to go back to first gear. The gearbox is automatic by default; shifting by hand switches it to manual.

What to watch

Tyres: bar = vertical load, colour = grip used (green → red at the limit), with live camber and slip angle. Balance: front vs rear slip angle, so you can see understeer or oversteer. g-g diagram: how much of the grip circle you use. Lap trace: compare your speed with your best lap.
